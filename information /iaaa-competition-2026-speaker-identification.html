IAAA Competition 2026: Speaker Identification

Open-Set Speaker Classification Challenge

Overview

In this challenge, you will build models that classify short audio chunks by speaker identity, while explicitly handling out-of-distribution (OOD) speakers.

We provide audio from 1,000 distinct people with different accents and recording conditions. For each person:

  • Roughly 50% of their audio is used for training.

  • The remaining 50% is held out for evaluation.

We define:

  • 447 “known” speakers: labeled as classes 1–447.

  • 553 “unknown / OOD” speakers: all grouped into a single “unknown speaker” class labeled 0.

Participants must train models that classify each audio chunk into 448 classes:

  • 0 = unknown / OOD speaker

  • 1–447 = specific known speakers

The goal is twofold:

  • Achieve high accuracy on the known speakers (1–447).

  • Correctly assign OOD speakers (never seen as distinct identities) to the “unknown” class 0.

Problem Description

Task

Given a short audio segment (chunk), your model must output a probability distribution over 448 classes:

  • ( p_0 ) – probability that the chunk belongs to the unknown (OOD) group.

  • ( p_1, p_2, \dots, p_{447} ) – probabilities for each of the 447 known speakers.

For evaluation, we:

  • Convert your probabilities to a predicted label via argmax.

  • Compare those labels to the ground-truth labels (0–447).

  • Compute:

    • A multi-class metric to judge speaker classification quality.

    • An OOD metric to judge how well your model distinguishes unknown speakers (class 0) from known speakers (1–447).

Dataset

Speakers and splits

Total known speakers: 447

For each speaker:

  • Audio is split into training and evaluation chunks, approximately 50/50:

    • Training chunks are provided to you.

    • Evaluation chunks are kept hidden by the organizers.

Training labels

For each training audio chunk, we provide:

  • A speaker label in {0, 1, ..., 447}:

    • Labels 1–447: individual known speakers.

    • Label 0: audio belonging to any of the unknown speakers. All of these speakers are collapsed into this single class.

This means you can train your model as a standard 448-way classifier, where class 0 is intentionally broad and heterogeneous: it represents “some speaker that is not one of the known identities.”

Evaluation labels

On the evaluation set:

  • For audio from known speakers (1–447), labels remain 1–447.

  • For audio from unknown speakers, labels are 0.

You never see which of the OOD persons a specific “0” label corresponds to. The core challenge is to assign 1–447 accurately, and group OOD speakers as 0.

Use of External Data

You may:

  • Use publicly available pretrained audio models.

  • Use external speech datasets.

  • Perform pretraining or self-supervised learning.

You must not:

  • Use labeled data containing evaluation speakers.

  • Access hidden evaluation labels.

Rules

Model behavior

Your model must produce a 448-way probability distribution for each chunk (classes 0–447).

Ensembling

Ensembling multiple models is allowed.
